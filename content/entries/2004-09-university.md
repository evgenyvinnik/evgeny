Five years of computer science: compilers, distributed systems, and a
dormitory network held together with borrowed switches.

The first two years were mathematics with a compiler attached. Discrete
structures, linear algebra, and a C++ course taught by someone who clearly
resented the standard library.

The third year was the useful one. An operating systems module where we
wrote a scheduler, a networks module where we wrote a TCP stack badly, and a
database module that has quietly informed every schema I have designed
since.

The dormitory network was the real curriculum. Forty machines, two borrowed
switches, one uplink, and a rotating cast of people who would unplug
something at three in the morning. I learned more about failure modes there
than in any lecture.
